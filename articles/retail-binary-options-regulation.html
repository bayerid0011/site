The Retail Binary Ban: What UK and European Regulatory Restrictions Actually Mean

The UK prohibition is permanent; EU treatment should be verified nationally rather than described as one permanent EU-wide rule.

By Financial Safety Research TeamLast reviewed: October 2026 · 8–11 min read
THE PROBLEM

Binary options have been subject to strong product intervention because of their risk characteristics and conduct concerns. The FCA permanently prohibited sale, marketing and distribution to UK retail consumers from April 2019. ESMA previously used temporary EU-wide measures, after which national authorities took their own approaches.

Anatomy of the mechanism

UK: permanent FCA prohibition

A firm offering binary options to UK retail consumers should be checked against the FCA register and warning list.

EU: verify the national rule

Do not describe historic ESMA temporary measures as identical permanent law in every member state.

Provider location is not the only issue

Marketing into a consumer's jurisdiction can engage local rules even when the provider is incorporated elsewhere.

Product labels can change

Some providers rebrand short-duration fixed-payout products. Review economic substance, not just marketing names.

Legal and regulatory analysis

Applicable framework

The FCA permanently prohibited the sale, marketing and distribution of binary options to UK retail consumers from April 2019. ESMA previously imposed EU-wide temporary product-intervention measures; current EU treatment should be checked with the relevant national regulator rather than described as one permanent EU-wide rule.

What the rule does not mean

A product restriction or unauthorised provider can be important evidence, but it does not automatically determine a card or bank outcome. Payment-network and banking processes have their own conditions.

Practical compliance point

Execution allegations should be tied to the exact underlying, expiry timestamp, price source and contract terms. A losing trade alone is not evidence of price manipulation.

Evidence that matters

Build a chronological file another person can follow without relying on memory. Preserve originals where possible.

  • Consumer jurisdiction
  • Provider entity/domain
  • Official register/warning
  • Product structure
  • Payment method

Practical next steps

  • Verify the current national position.
  • Preserve the primary source.
  • Separate regulation from payment-dispute mechanics.
  • Report unauthorised marketing through official channels.

What not to do

  • Do not claim one identical EU rule without checking.
  • Do not promise card reversal solely because a product is restricted.
  • Do not rely on provider self-certification.
