The Name Mismatch Problem: What Verification of Payee Changes — and What It Does Not

A name/IBAN check can warn of mismatches, but liability still depends on the payment context, warnings and rules.

By Financial Safety Research TeamLast reviewed: October 2026 · 8–11 min read
THE PROBLEM

A payer believes money is going to Company A, but the beneficiary is Company B or an individual. That mismatch can be important evidence. The EU Instant Payments Regulation introduced verification-of-payee requirements with phased implementation, supported by the EPC Verification of Payee scheme.

Anatomy of the mechanism

Record the name entered

Keep beneficiary name, IBAN and any bank warning shown before authorisation.

Separate no-match from an override

If the bank displayed a mismatch and the payer proceeded, that fact matters to later analysis.

Check the implementation scope

Verification obligations have phased dates and scope; not every historic or non-euro payment is treated identically.

Compare payee with the contract

A mismatch is stronger when the merchant represented that payment would go to a specific regulated entity.

Preserve the confirmation screens

The exact warning or match wording can be important evidence.

Legal and regulatory analysis

Applicable framework

PSD2 remains the current EU payment-services framework as of October 2026. PSD3 and the Payment Services Regulation reached political agreement and are progressing toward final adoption; this site does not treat the future framework as already in force.

What the rule does not mean

The EPC SEPA Credit Transfer scheme contains a formal Recall procedure for defined reasons. The originator PSP initiates it; a recall request is not a guarantee that funds will be returned.

Practical compliance point

AML and sanctions controls are public-law compliance duties, not a universal private reimbursement guarantee for every sender of an authorised transfer.

Evidence that matters

Build a chronological file another person can follow without relying on memory. Preserve originals where possible.

  • Entered beneficiary name
  • IBAN/account
  • VOP result/warning
  • Contract/invoice payee
  • Authorisation screen

Practical next steps

  • Save mismatch screenshots.
  • Tell the bank exactly what warning appeared.
  • Compare payee with the contract.
  • Use the bank complaint route for review.

What not to do

  • Do not claim every mismatch proves fraud.
  • Do not assume a match verifies the investment.
  • Do not rewrite the bank warning after the event.
