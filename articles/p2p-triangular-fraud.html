The P2P Ledger Problem: How Triangular Fraud Can Pull an Innocent Seller Into a Bank Investigation

Why the payer, exchange counterparty and crypto recipient may be three different people — and what records reduce confusion.

By Financial Safety Research TeamLast reviewed: October 2026 · 8–11 min read
THE PROBLEM

In a triangular P2P fraud, a criminal can persuade a third-party victim to transfer money to a legitimate crypto seller. The seller sees the payment and releases crypto to the fraudster's exchange account or wallet. Later the bank payment is disputed and the seller appears to be the beneficiary of the fraud.

Anatomy of the mechanism

Map the three roles

Identify the bank payer, the P2P account placing the order and the wallet or exchange account receiving crypto. Do not assume they are the same person.

Preserve the exchange order

Order ID, username, timestamps, chat and release confirmation show why the seller believed payment was genuine.

Preserve the bank record

Keep the payer name, reference and time. A third-party payment can be a warning under platform rules.

Trace the crypto release

Record the on-platform transfer or blockchain transaction to show where value moved after the bank payment.

Answer the bank chronologically

Explain the P2P sale and provide records rather than making unsupported accusations about the original payer.

Legal and regulatory analysis

Applicable framework

PSD2 remains the current EU payment-services framework as of October 2026. PSD3 and the Payment Services Regulation reached political agreement and are progressing toward final adoption; this site does not treat the future framework as already in force.

What the rule does not mean

The EPC SEPA Credit Transfer scheme contains a formal Recall procedure for defined reasons. The originator PSP initiates it; a recall request is not a guarantee that funds will be returned.

Practical compliance point

AML and sanctions controls are public-law compliance duties, not a universal private reimbursement guarantee for every sender of an authorised transfer.

Evidence that matters

Build a chronological file another person can follow without relying on memory. Preserve originals where possible.

  • P2P order ID
  • Counterparty profile
  • Bank payer/reference
  • Crypto release record
  • Platform chat/dispute ticket

Practical next steps

  • Export the full order record.
  • Respond promptly to bank information requests.
  • Report the counterparty through the exchange.
  • Keep communication factual.

What not to do

  • Do not contact the bank payer aggressively.
  • Do not fabricate invoices.
  • Do not move funds to evade a bank review.
